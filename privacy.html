Privacy policy

For the app The Fines Box (Klubkassen). Last changed 17 November 2026.

Who is responsible

The Fines Box is made and run by ShieldCastle Labs (Søren Børsting Skjoldborg), Denmark, who is the data controller. Write to contact@thefinesbox.com about anything to do with your information.

The team's role and ours

ShieldCastle Labs decides how the app works and is responsible for the information in it: your account, login, notifications, crash reports, reports, backups and the team's information while it is stored in the app.

Each team decides for itself how it uses the app: which fines it has, who gets a fine, what the fees are, who is picked and which payments are recorded. The team (its admins, fines judges, coaches and treasurers) is responsible for those decisions and for what the team does with the information outside the app, for example when a treasurer keeps the team's accounts or someone shares a picture of the ranking. If you disagree with something the team has recorded about you, such as a fine or a payment, talk to the team's admins or fines judges first. You can always write to us as well, and we'll help.

What we store

  • Your account: your email address, the name you type, the app's language, and how you log in (code by email, Apple or Google). If you log in with Apple and choose "Hide My Email", we only get an address from Apple that forwards to you.
  • Your teams: which teams you're on, your roles (e.g. admin or fines judge), requests to join, and the team's name, logo, colours, motto, what the fines box is for, and links.
  • Fines: the team's list of fines, fine proposals, approved fines, corrections and comments, and who gave or proposed them. If you send an anonymous proposal, only the team's fines judges can see that it was you.
  • Matches and trainings: the team's matches and trainings, whether you can play (and when you changed it, with your comment), whether you've signed up for a training, whether you're picked and your place in the line-up, when you were checked in (yourself, by a coach, or automatically 1 hour before when you're picked or signed up), and whether you're exempt from the fee per session or the season fee. If the team has switched on automatic fines, a late cancellation or a no-show can become a fine proposal to the team's fines judges.
  • Player of the match: after a match, everyone who was checked in can vote for the player of the match. While the vote is open we keep your vote so you can change it; nobody on the team can see who you voted for. When the result is found we delete who voted for whom and keep only the number of votes per player and who won, which the whole team can see. If the team has switched it on, the player of the match can get a fine proposal.
  • Days away: the days you've added when you can't play, and your note if you write one. Only you and your teams' coaches see the days and the note; the rest of the team only sees that you can't play.
  • Guest players: if a coach adds a guest, we keep the guest's name, check-in, fees and payments on the team. A guest has no account in the app.
  • What you owe and have paid: your fines, fees for matches and trainings and the season fee (for the whole season, or for autumn and spring), the payments you report ("I've paid": amount, MobilePay or cash, a short reference and who got the cash), the MobilePay box you paid to, the treasurer's confirmation, payments a treasurer registers for you, and cash a member holds for the team, hands on or spends. The app never moves money and stores no card or bank details: you pay in MobilePay or in cash.
  • Suggestions: suggestions for the fine library and for translations of the team's fines.
  • Reports: if you report content, what you reported, a copy of what it said, and your own words.
  • Seasons: the team's seasons, so the ranking of fines and player of the match can be shown per season. It is worked out from the information above and only shown to your team.
  • Notifications: if you turn notifications on, your phone's push address, the messages the app has sent you (e.g. "New fine" or a reminder to answer), and which kinds of message you've switched off. When you log out, we forget the phone's address.
  • Crash reports: if the app crashes, a report about the error: which screen, the phone model, and the iOS and app versions. Without your name, email or the team's fines.

We don't use ads, tracking or analytics tools, and we don't sell information.

Why, and on what basis

We use the information to give you and your team the app: log you in, show the team's fines, ranking, members, matches and accounts, send you login codes and, if you want them, notifications. The basis is that this is necessary to provide the service you asked for (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)). Technical logs, e.g. IP addresses at login, and crash reports are used for security and fixing errors (Article 6(1)(f), our legitimate interest in a secure app that works).

Who can see it

What you do on a team can be seen by the others on the team: your name, your roles, the team's fines and match fees, the ranking, the matches, who can't play, the squad and line-up once a coach has published them, and who has checked in. Your payments and what you owe in total are private: only you, the team's treasurers and whoever received your cash can see them. A report is seen by us only. Each team only sees its own information.

A teammate can share a picture of the ranking or the line-up outside the team, for example in a group chat. The picture is made on their phone and shows names and amounts or places; the app doesn't send it anywhere, and whoever shares it is responsible for where it goes.

When you pay with MobilePay you leave the app. MobilePay handles the payment under its own terms, and the person who owns the team's MobilePay box sees your name and phone number there, as with any MobilePay payment.

We use these suppliers (data processors):

  • Supabase – database, login and images. Servers in Ireland (EU).
  • Resend – sends login codes by email. Sent from Ireland (EU).
  • Expo (USA) – delivers app updates, and passes notifications on to Apple. Sees your phone's IP address and the app's version when the app checks for an update, and a notification's text and push address when it's sent.
  • Sentry – crash reports. Servers in Germany (EU).
  • Apple and Google – login, only if you choose to log in with them. Apple also delivers notifications to your iPhone.
  • GitHub (USA) – shows this website, and keeps an encrypted backup of the database each night for 30 days. GitHub can't read it.

Suppliers in the USA are used under the EU's rules for transferring information (the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or the EU's standard contractual clauses).

How long

We keep the information for as long as you have an account. You can delete the account in the app under Profile → Delete account. Your email, name and login methods are then deleted. Fines, fees and payments stay in the team's history as "Former player" without your name, so the team's accounts still add up. If you leave a team, your fines stay in the team's history. Backups are deleted after 30 days, so a deleted account is gone from them within 30 days too.

Your rights

You have the right to see the information we hold about you, have it corrected or deleted, get a copy of it, restrict its use and object. Write to contact@thefinesbox.com. You can also complain to the Danish Data Protection Agency (datatilsynet.dk).

Age

The app is for people aged 13 and over. If you're under 18, you need a parent's or guardian's permission to create and use an account.

Changes

If we change the policy, we update the date at the top, and we tell you about bigger changes in the app.
